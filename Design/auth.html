Flow 1.3 — guided activation

The hardest problem in this product

The number is the account, so there's no email and no password to fall back on. M-Pesa and WhatsApp have exactly the same shape, and both answer it the same way: they lean on the carrier, who already checked your ID when they issued the SIM.

Flow 1.2 · atlas F-05

Sign-up, sign-in and recovery

Five screens, no email, no password, no card, target under two minutes. Everything below traces either to atlas Flow 05 or to a piece of evidence found while designing it. Where the two disagreed, the disagreement is written down rather than quietly resolved.

Nothing is written to the database until screen 5. A verified OTP creates a session, not an account — so an abandoned signup leaves no orphan tenant, no half-consent, and no record we would then have to justify holding. The number is remembered for 30 days so a return doesn't restart from zero, and that is the only trace.

02

The decision I changed my mind about

The design system shipped with a six-box OTP field. It was wrong, and both this flow and the design system have been corrected.

What broke

Paste fills one box

The WCAG 2.2 SC 3.3.8 Understanding document uses a six-box OTP as its named failure example, for exactly this reason. A code that must be transcribed by hand rather than pasted fails the criterion.

What broke

iOS autofill has nowhere to go

Safari's autocomplete="one-time-code" inserts the whole code into the focused field. There is no protocol for distributing six characters across six separate controls, so it lands in box one or nowhere.

What broke

Six fields, not one code

A screen reader announces six unrelated inputs. Auto-advance fights the virtual cursor. The user has to track their own position with no way to know how many digits remain.

What we do now

One input, six painted slots

A single <input> with autocomplete="one-time-code", laid over six aria-hidden slots with the caret drawn into the active one. GOV.UK reaches the same place with letter-spacing. Paste, autofill, backspace and arrow keys are all native and free.

03

Getting the code to arrive

This is the step the flow lives or dies on, in a market where A2P SMS delivery is genuinely unreliable and nobody is told when a message is dropped.

ChannelOfferedWhy there, why then
SMSImmediatelyThe only channel that needs nothing installed. Sender ID must be registered separately with MTN and Airtel — they do not share a registration
WhatsAppAfter 30s, and automatically after two failed SMSRides IP, not the interconnect, so it survives a bad SMS route. Meta does not bill undelivered messages. Twilio's own A/B tests found 20–40% of users pick WhatsApp when offered. Not primary, because it isn't necessarily installed on that SIM
Voice read-outAfter 60s and two SMS attemptsWorks with no data at all. Gated because an ungated voice fallback is an amplifier for toll fraud — Twilio's guidance is to hold it behind three failed attempts
A human on WhatsAppFrom the recovery hub, alwaysSomeone whose number simply cannot receive anything must still be able to reach a person

A disagreement worth recording. Atlas Flow 05 says voice fallback after 60 seconds. Twilio's abuse guidance says hold it behind three failed attempts, because an ungated voice channel is a toll-fraud amplifier. This design takes both: 60 seconds and two SMS attempts, whichever is later. If pilot data shows that costs real signups, the gate loosens — but it starts closed, because the cost of the alternative lands on us in a currency we can't refuse to pay.

No autofill magic for the fallbacks. The WebOTP API parses SMS only — its transport option accepts nothing else. A code delivered by voice or WhatsApp must be typed. That is not a footnote: it means manual entry is the majority path for the users most likely to need help, so the field is sized and spaced for typing, not for autofill.

04

Abuse, and why there is no CAPTCHA

SMS pumping is the attack this endpoint attracts: an attacker triggers OTPs to number ranges in high-termination-rate markets and takes a cut of the interconnect fee. No account is ever compromised — we simply pay for the traffic. Twilio reports blocking over $62M in customer losses since 2022; X claimed $60M a year at its peak.

A CAPTCHA on the first screen would be the wrong trade. It taxes every legitimate salon owner on a 2G connection in order to inconvenience a bot that can solve it anyway. Geo-restriction alone removes most of this attack surface for a product that serves exactly one country, and ratio monitoring catches what's left. Friction is held in reserve for the anomaly, not spent on the default.

05

Consent, and why one checkbox would be invalid

Rwanda's Law 058/2021 is modelled closely on the GDPR, and the EDPB's guidance on this is not ambiguous: where processing serves more than one purpose, the person must be free to choose which they accept. Bundling them means consent is presumed not freely given.

Required · contract

Terms and DPA

The legal basis here is contract necessity, not consent — you cannot have an account without it, and pretending it is a free choice would be dishonest about what it is.

Optional · consent

Product news on WhatsApp

Genuinely separate, unticked, and the account behaves identically without it. Withdrawal is a single word, STOP, through the same channel it was given in.

Not here

Recording · voice cloning · your customers' opt-in

Different purposes, and in two cases a different person entirely. Each is asked at the moment it becomes relevant.

Every consent event writes a record that can be produced years later. The ICO's list of what must be kept is the shape of it: who, when, what they were shown, how, and whether it has since been withdrawn.

consent_event {
  subject: "+250788123456",
  purpose: "terms_and_dpa" | "marketing_whatsapp",
  granted: true,
  document_version: "1.0", wording_hash: "sha256:…",
  language_shown: "rw",
  channel: "web_signup", ip_country: "RW",
  at: "2026-09-04T09:41:07Z", withdrawn_at: null
}

language_shown is not bookkeeping. Consent must be informed, and a person cannot be informed by a document in a language they read less comfortably. That single requirement is why the language screen sits at step four and the terms at step five, and why the record stores which language the wording was actually presented in.

06

Recovery — the genuinely hard part

The number is the account. There is no email and no password to fall back on, which is the same corner M-Pesa, WhatsApp, Telegram and Signal all painted themselves into. Three paths, in descending order of how often they happen.

SituationWhat we doWhy
Number works, code isn't arrivingWhatsApp, then voice, then a person. No identity check at allNine times in ten this is a delivery problem, not an identity problem, and treating it as one insults the user
SIM or handset lostSend them to MTN or Airtel to reissue the SIM against their national ID, then a normal codeThe carrier already runs a real identity check at the counter. Ours would be weaker and slower. This is exactly what WhatsApp and M-Pesa do
Number changed permanentlyHuman review: national ID plus a photo holding it, RDB certificate if there is one, last MoMo top-up amount and date, and one fact only the owner could knowThe only case with no automatic answer. Atlas Flow 21 already routes it to Super Admin with identity verification of both parties, logged
Shared handsetAn optional account PIN, offered after first value, never at signupPossession of the phone doesn't identify the person when a family shares it. Signal and WhatsApp both solve this with an opt-in second factor, and both are right

The optional PIN also earns a seven-day hold. With it set, re-registering this number on a new device starts a timer before the new registration takes effect — Signal's registration-lock pattern. It is the only defence against a SIM swap that exists for an account whose whole identity is a phone number, and this account controls a business's phone line and its prepaid balance. It is off by default because a lock a user doesn't understand is a lock they get trapped behind.

Open question, carried forward from atlas 21.d. What identity evidence is actually sufficient in a market where 87% of enterprises are informal and many have no RDB certificate at all? The evidence list above is a starting position, not a settled policy, and the "one fact only you would know" row is doing more work than it should have to.

08

Where I went against the evidence, and why

Kept, against the grain

The five-mark progress bar

GOV.UK's one-thing-per-page guidance doesn't call for a step counter, and Zuko's data finds no strong link between field count and completion. But five screens is long enough that a shape which fills answers "how much more of this is there" — so the marks stay and the words "step 2 of 5" go. Progress without arithmetic.

Dropped

Auto-submit on the sixth digit

Tempting, and wrong here. On a slow connection a user mid-correction has six characters in the field for a moment before backspacing. Auto-submit turns that into a false "wrong code", which is worse than one extra tap. There is a Continue button and it is always the thing that submits.

Dropped

Asking for an email "just in case"

It's the obvious hedge against the recovery problem in section 06, and it is a lie: an email address collected at signup by someone who doesn't use email is an address that won't work when it's needed, bought with a field that measurably costs completions.
