What we do when the window is closing

If a conversation isn't finished and the 24 hours are nearly up, Subiza offers to move that customer to WhatsApp or SMS. Without that, the conversation simply dies and you never learn you lost someone.

Why we don't use the human-agent tag

It would extend the window from 24 hours to 7 days, and the API would happily let us set it. But Meta's policy reserves it for messages a real person actually sends. An AI agent using it would be against the rules even though nothing would stop it technically — so we don't.

Instagram is the strictest of the three

There is no paid way past the 24 hours, and a private reply to a comment can be sent exactly once, ever. Almost no product surfaces either, and both are how businesses silently lose people.

Not idempotent, so we make it so

A second private reply to the same comment returns a hard error, not a quiet no-op. So we record that we've replied before we ever retry — the platform won't protect us, so the product has to.

Silent disconnection is the worst failure here

Neither Meta nor Instagram pushes a revocation notice. We find out from the first failed send, mark the channel down within five minutes, and tell you — because a channel that quietly stops is a business losing customers with no idea it's happening.

One channel down never touches the others

Flow 1.5 · atlas F-08

Messaging channel connection

Four channels, four completely different gatekeepers. The design job is to represent each platform's real requirements, real waiting and real limits — because a connection flow that hides any of the three produces a customer who feels misled in week two.

01

Four things the research changed

Telegram · new

BotFather is no longer the only way

Bot API 9.6, around April 2026, added managed bots: our bot sends a peer request, the owner approves it with one tap, and we then pull the token via bots.exportBotToken. No chat with BotFather, no copying a secret by hand. The owner's live consent is still required and always will be — what's gone is the transcription, not the permission. BotFather stays as the fallback for older clients.

Instagram · new

No Facebook Page needed

The Instagram API with Instagram Login authenticates against Instagram directly — scopes instagram_business_basic and instagram_business_manage_messages, no linked Page. The older Messenger-Platform route still demands one. For salon owners who have an Instagram account and no Page, that removes the step that used to end the flow.

Corrected

Private replies are not idempotent

The atlas said “idempotent, never retried.” A second private reply to the same comment returns a hard error, not a quiet no-op — so the platform will not protect us. The product must record that it replied before any retry can happen. The behaviour is right; the reason was wrong.

Corrected

1,000 free service messages is per number, not per WABA

And it arrives on 1 October 2026, alongside in-window utility templates losing their free status. For a salon replying to a few dozen customers a month this changes nothing — but it is real, it is soon, and the connected screen says so before a bill does.

02

The AI ban, read properly

Every tenant will have heard that Meta banned AI on WhatsApp. The actual clause, from the WhatsApp Business Solution Terms:

“Providers and developers of artificial intelligence… are strictly prohibited from accessing or using the WhatsApp Business Solution… for the purposes of providing, delivering, offering, selling, or otherwise making available such technologies when such technologies are the primary (rather than incidental or ancillary) functionality being made available for use.”

What is bannedWhat is not
WhatsApp as a distribution channel for a general-purpose assistant — a chatbot people talk to for its own sakeA business whose product is haircuts, using an agent to answer questions about its own hours, prices and bookings. This is the “incidental or ancillary” case the clause carves out
Two nuances worth knowing. It is not a flat global ban — the terms carve out numbers registered in the EEA and Brazil, where Meta instead charges AI providers per message rather than blocking them. And the widely-cited 15 January 2026 enforcement date is well corroborated across independent reporting but does not appear verbatim in the terms text itself

So we say it on the connected screen, in Meta's own words. A tenant who has half-heard about a ban and gets no answer will assume the worst — and the honest answer here is genuinely reassuring, which makes hiding it doubly foolish.

03

The payment wall, and the way through it

Meta bills the business, not the software vendor. In a market with almost no card penetration that is a conversion wall, and atlas question 8.b flagged it as unresolved. The research found the resolution, and it is a partner-tier decision rather than a design one.

TierBilling relationshipWhat it means for a Kigali salon
Tech Provider“Clients onboarded by Tech Providers must provide their own payment method after onboarding is complete. Meta will then bill these clients”Every owner needs an international card. Most don't have one. The channel is effectively closed to them
Solution PartnerHolds a credit line with Meta, can consolidate billing and invoice clients directlyWe pay Meta, the owner pays us by MoMo, and no card is ever needed

This is the single most consequential commercial finding in this flow. Building directly as a Tech Provider puts a card wall in front of the highest-value channel in the market. Building on a BSP that already holds Solution Partner status removes it entirely. It is worth confirming the tier distinction directly with whichever BSP we evaluate — the source for it is a partner's documentation rather than Meta's own partner pages, which could not be loaded — but the direction is clear and the stakes are high enough to check properly.

07

The human-agent tag: a rule we impose on ourselves

Instagram's human-agent tag extends the reply window from 24 hours to 7 days, and it is set with an API parameter — nothing technical stops software from sending it. But Meta's policy reserves it for messages a live human actually initiates. An AI receptionist using it would be breaking the rules while the API happily complied. So Subiza does not use it, and the window-closing migration offer exists precisely because the easy shortcut is off limits.
