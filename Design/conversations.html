The decision this flow turns on

Channel is metadata, not structure. Marie called on Tuesday and messaged on Thursday — that is one relationship.

Voice products keep call logs and chat products keep threads, and nobody joins them. Which is why there is one list here, and the channel is a 13px glyph inside the row rather than a tab above it.

This is not a to-do list

Most conversations need nothing. Presenting fifty handled conversations as fifty items to process is manufactured work — and a product that manufactures work gets closed. What needs her goes on top; everything else is summarised.

The summary is written, not the first line

“Asked about prices, then booked Saturday” can be read in a second. “Muraho, ndashaka…” cannot. Two minutes a day is only possible if one line is enough.

Searching what was said

An owner remembers “the woman who asked about Saturday”, not a phone number. Searching the transcript is what matches how people actually remember a conversation.

The trust mechanism

An owner who can see that 15,000 came from row 3 of her own price list stops worrying that it invents things.

Per-answer citation is essentially absent across this category. It is the strongest trust mechanism available and it is not expensive to build.

Seven kinds of citation

A price row · opening hours · a rule · an answer you wrote · composed from several · it didn't know · we cannot say. An answer read from a typed row and an answer composed from three sources are not the same claim, and rendering them identically would flatten the only distinction that matters.

The seventh keeps the other six honest

If we render a citation on 100% of turns, some of them will be post-hoc rationalisation. So where we cannot attribute a sentence, we say so rather than manufacture a source. The one amber chip in a hundred is what makes the other ninety-nine worth believing.

Thumbs-down writes the answer

A thumbs-down that only registers displeasure is a dead signal. This one asks a single question — what should it have said — and writes the answer into its knowledge. Time spent reading becomes time spent improving.

The 24 hours

Blocked before she types — never a silent failure after she sends.

Writing a long reply and then getting an error is the worst failure mode this product has: the time is spent, and the customer never hears from her.

The countdown is visible before it matters

Over 21 hours it is a quiet line. Under two hours it turns amber. Once it has passed, the box is replaced by what she can do instead — not by a box she cannot type in.

The thing we don't build

The atlas says that when an Instagram window closes we should offer to migrate the customer to WhatsApp. We don't. Following somebody onto another channel uninvited is stalking, and it is also how Instagram's own platform policy reads it. Instead we show what is actually allowed: call her, or wait for her to write.

A wrong merge is worse than no merge

A wrong merge shows one customer another's history. It cannot be undone in their memory.

So the phone number is the key — it covers voice, WhatsApp and SMS. Instagram and Messenger link only when the customer volunteers a number. A weak signal is never merged automatically.

What a weak signal gets instead

It is neither merged nor discarded. It is offered: here is what matched, here is what didn't, you decide. The decision is hers because she is the one who knows her customers, and it is reversible.

When no number arrives

Call forwarding sometimes strips the caller's number and we cannot change that. So we say exactly that — the conversation is complete and true, we simply cannot join it to another — rather than showing an empty timeline that reads as a bug.

Taking over

That it is possible matters more than that it is used.

This is the button an owner looks for before agreeing to put an agent in front of her customers. Most never press it. Its being there is what got them to say yes.

Where this flow stops

Take over starts here and finishes in Flow 15. This screen renders the button and what it will do; the handover itself — how the agent stops, what the caller hears, how she writes afterwards — belongs to that flow, which is the next one.

Two people, one conversation

If a colleague already has it open, she sees that before she writes. Two people answering one customer is worse than nobody answering.

When something is missing

A missing recording is a missing recording — not a broken player.

Each of these says something different about whether her customers can still reach her. Collapsing them into one “something went wrong” would waste the only distinction that matters.

Complete versus incomplete

A transcript without audio is still complete. Showing it as broken would make her distrust it for no reason — what she wants is what was said, and that is there.

Flow 1.12 · atlas F-14 · daily — the habit loop

Conversations

The unified voice and chat inbox — the IA idea the atlas says nobody in the category has shipped. Two minutes a day, and the place where trust is either accumulated or lost, one conversation at a time.

01

Two positions, and everything follows

Channel is metadata, not structure. Marie called on Tuesday and messaged on Thursday; that is one relationship, and it is how a shop owner thinks. So there is one list, and the channel is a 13px glyph inside the row rather than a tab above it. The moment channel becomes structure, the product becomes two products sharing a login.

The Inbox is not a to-do list. Most conversations need nothing. Fifty handled conversations presented as fifty items to process is manufactured work, and a product that manufactures work gets closed. Target session: under two minutes.

02

Seven kinds of citation, and why the seventh exists

Atlas §4.2 calls zone 4 “the single strongest trust mechanism available, and largely absent from the category”, and SC 14.5 demands a trace on 100% of AI turns. The trap is that a citation rendered on every turn is a citation that will sometimes be invented — a post-hoc source attached to a sentence the model composed. That is worse than no citation, because it spends credibility rather than building it.

SC 14.5 is satisfied literally — every AI turn carries a trace — while staying honest, because one of the trace's possible values is we cannot account for this sentence. That chip is amber, it names what it is not (not the price list, not a rule, not an answer she wrote), and it offers the thumbs-down. The one amber chip in a hundred is what makes the other ninety-nine worth reading.

This is the same move as language.html's fourth quality rung and golive.html's Couldn't judge. A product that can say “I don't know” about its own behaviour is the only kind whose confident claims mean anything.

03

The window, and one thing the atlas asks for that we won't build

W6 and I2 give a 24-hour customer-service window. The failure this must prevent is not a rejected send — it is a long reply typed into a box that was never going to work. So the window state is on screen before the composer, the countdown is quiet above 21 hours and amber under two, and when it has passed the composer is replaced by what she can do instead rather than disabled in place.

Recorded deviation. Atlas §6 (I4) says that when an Instagram window is closing on an unresolved conversation we should “offer to migrate the customer to WhatsApp or SMS”. This file does not build that. Moving somebody onto a channel they did not choose, to get around a limit that exists to protect them, is following a customer around — and Instagram's platform policy reads it the same way. The alternatives offered are the two that are genuinely available: call her, or wait for her to write again. If the pilot shows owners need more, the answer is to ask the customer for permission inside the open window, not to route around the closed one.

04

Identity: what merges, what is offered, what never merges

SC 14.9 is zero wrong identity merges, and the reason is in atlas §4.4: a wrong merge exposes one customer's history to another. That is a privacy failure, and unlike a missing link it cannot be undone in the customer's memory.

The suggestion surface shows its own evidence — two green ticks and one amber cross — because “these might be the same person” with no reasons is a request to guess. And the answer No, different people is a real answer that is remembered, not a dismissal that returns next week.

05

The list, and why the summary is written

A row is: who · channel glyph · time · a generated one-line summary · an outcome chip. The summary is generated, never the first line of the transcript. “Asked about braid prices, then booked Saturday at nine” can be read in a second. “Muraho, ndashaka…” cannot, and two minutes a day is only achievable if one line is enough.

Five outcomes — Booked · Answered · Message taken · Needs you · Escalated — and only one of them is a task. Items that need her sit at the top regardless of recency, because recency is not urgency.

Search covers name, number, and what was said, with the matched phrase highlighted in the row. An owner remembers “the woman who asked about Saturday”, not a number.

06

Responsive: the split is a redesign, not a reflow

Above 1024 the list and the detail sit side by side — a 320px list beside a detail pane, which is the shape of a scan-and-open loop. Below, they are two views with a back button, and the split does not narrow: a 320px list next to a detail on a 360px screen is two unreadable columns where one readable one would do.

The four actions wrap two-by-two rather than shrinking; four buttons at 360 would each fall under the tap-target floor. The filters scroll horizontally rather than wrapping onto a second row that would push the first conversation below the fold. The timeline date column narrows from 56 to 48px, which is the one place a pure reflow is correct.

07

The shell, inherited

This is the second surface to use the app shell home.html settled — .appframe, .appnav, .tabbar, .apphead with its two rows and three state chips, .pausebtn. None of it needed changing to fit here, which is the only real test of whether it was designed or merely drawn. The header's greeting slot takes the section name and a live count; everything else is untouched.

One inheritance trap, found by testing and recorded for the six flows that come next. A component's class rules travel when you lift them; its @media overrides do not. The first build of this file inherited .appframe.wide but not the 640px block that collapses it back to the phone form — so at 360px in desktop mode the rail appeared and the bottom bar vanished, which is exactly the layout nobody can use. The override is restated here. Any later flow reusing the shell must restate it too, or lift the media block deliberately.

10

The two-minute test

Notification → the list opens with two items above the fold → she reads a one-line summary and an outcome chip, and does not open the conversation → taps Call back on the first → it leaves the list, with Moved to Conversations-style accounting → the second is a price she can answer in the composer without leaving → done. Six taps, no scrolling required, no reading required. The transcript, the citations and the timeline are all there for the day she wants them, and none of them are on the path.

This is the design's actual claim: the trust machinery has to be present and off the critical path at the same time. A product that makes her read a transcript to close a loop will not be opened four days a week.

11

Open questions this file does not settle

Atlas 14.a — whether owners read transcripts or only outcomes — is exactly why the transcript is below the two zones that answer the question without it. 14.b, whether the cross-channel timeline is valued or merely liked by us, is a pilot question; the timeline is built but deliberately last on the screen. 14.d — whether the Inbox should be reachable through WhatsApp — is not drawn, and is a real scope question for Flow 25.

And the standing caveat: the Kinyarwanda is carefully written and has not been reviewed by a native speaker. Atlas SC 6.12 requires that walkthrough. The same standing gap applies as everywhere in this programme — two languages are wired, G15 asks for four.
