The decision this screen turns on

“About 11 days”, not “14,200 RWF”. A balance in francs makes her do arithmetic about her own usage.

Days remaining is the answer to the question she is asking — have I got enough? The francs are true, but they are a sum, and a sum about her own usage rather than our balance sheet.

The atlas leads with the francs

Its §3 sketch puts 14,200 RWF at the top. But §4.1 argues the opposite, at length — and that is the half we followed. This file implements the reasoning rather than the drawing.

No card, anywhere

5.8 million active MoMo users in Rwanda; card penetration is marginal. A card field anywhere in this product is a conversion wall, not a lesser option.

The one place a PIN is asked for

That prompt comes from MTN. Subiza will never ask for your PIN.

MoMo fraud in this market is endemic, and this is the only flow in the product that legitimately causes a PIN prompt. So it names where the prompt comes from and what we will never do — because staying quiet about it is how somebody else gets to impersonate us.

Pending is a real state

MoMo settles asynchronously, not instantly. Pretending otherwise is how a payment gets lost. So pending stays pending, carries a way to check, and never resolves into silence.

We never touch the money

The collection goes through a licensed aggregator. That keeps Subiza outside BNR payment-service-provider licensing — and it means your money is never sitting on our trustworthiness.

A mechanism, not a promise

We never remove the forwarding. Subiza declines the call, and the carrier falls through to her.

A promise with a mechanism can be believed; one without is marketing. And this is the most important promise in the flow: a business must never be worse off for having tried us.

Three warnings before anything

A fifth left · two days left · zero with 72 hours of grace. Losing a customer to a lapsed balance is the most avoidable kind of churn there is — and it is a design problem, not a payments one.

Our outage cannot pause her

When paying is impossible because of us, the grace extends automatically. Punishing a customer for our failure is indefensible, and it should not depend on somebody remembering — it has to be automatic.

Downgrade is one tap

Exactly as upgrading is one tap. No second confirmation, no list of what you'd lose, no hoping she forgets. A dark pattern buys one month and costs a reputation — and in this market reputation travels fast.

Cost and value, in one document

A bill alone invites cancellation. A bill beside “89 you'd have missed” invites renewal.

The cost on top, always. Leading with the value would be an attempt to soften the number, and she would see it.

The number stays openable

The 89 is the same number Results reports, and every conversation behind it can be read. A figure on an invoice that cannot be checked is a figure that invites suspicion.

A ceiling she set herself

This category was built on contracts small businesses could not exit. A spend cap is the direct answer, and she sets it — rather than us granting her “flexibility”.

Flow 1.15 · atlas F-17 · blocks paid usage

Credit & mobile money

Paying the way this market already pays — and making sure that not paying never leaves a business harder to reach than it was before. The second half is the one that needed designing.

01

Days, not francs

Atlas §4.1: “‘About 11 days’ rather than ‘14,200 RWF’ is the important detail. A balance in francs requires the owner to do arithmetic about their own usage. Days remaining is the answer to the question they are actually asking.”

So days are the headline at 3.6rem and the francs are a supporting line below a rule. This inverts the atlas's own §3 sketch, which puts 14,200 RWF at the top of the card — and that is deliberate: §4.1 argues the case at length and §3 is a drawing. Where a document contradicts itself, the reasoning wins.

The same substitution runs through the top-up amounts: each preset is labelled ~ 8 days as well as 10,000, because the question how much should I add is really how long do I want to stop thinking about this.

02

The one place this product causes a PIN prompt

voice.html already ships the line “We will never ask you for your MoMo PIN, a code, or any money.” This flow is the single exception in the product where a PIN prompt legitimately appears — and it appears on her phone, from MTN, not from us.

MoMo fraud in this market is endemic, so that distinction is drawn rather than assumed. c2 carries two statements in one block: your phone will ask for your MoMo PIN — that prompt comes from MTN, and beneath it, in risk colour, Subiza will never ask for your PIN — not in this app, not in a message, not on a call. Anyone who asks is not us.

And c3 draws the MTN prompt itself — sender, amount, payee, the PIN field — so she knows what a real one looks like. Teaching somebody to recognise the genuine article is better fraud protection than telling them to be careful, and this is the only screen in the programme positioned to do it.

03

Pending is a state, not a spinner over an assumption

MoMo collection is asynchronous. The failure this must prevent is not a declined payment — it is a payment that succeeded while our screen said nothing, or said it had failed.

04

What lapsing means — a mechanism, not a promise

Atlas §4.3 is the most important behaviour in this flow and SC 17.5 tests it end-to-end at zero balance. c8b renders it as two columns, and the left one leads with the sentence the whole product rests on: calls ring your own phone as they always did, with the number.

But the columns are not the design. The mechanism is:

We never remove your forwarding. Subiza simply declines the call, so the carrier's no-answer rule falls through to you exactly as it did before Subiza existed.

A promise with a mechanism can be believed; one without it is marketing. And this particular mechanism has a second virtue — there is nothing for her to repair. No code to re-dial, no setting to restore, no support call. The state she returns to is the state she was in before she ever met us.

05

That last row is the one worth defending. Punishing a customer for a failure of ours is indefensible, and it should not depend on somebody remembering to be generous — c11 shows it as an automatic act with the new number on screen. SC 17.10 fault-injects it.

c7 also shows her the two warnings she already had, greyed, with their dates. Being able to see that you were told twice is what makes the third message land as information rather than as an ambush.

06

Downgrade is structurally as easy as upgrade

SC 17.8 is a UI audit: the downgrade path must have the same number of taps as the upgrade. So c8 makes the symmetry structural rather than promised — three plan cards, one button each, same size, same position, same wording (Move to this). The current plan's button is the only inert one.

Nothing dims the cheaper plan, nothing lists what she would lose, nothing asks twice. The copy states why: a dark pattern buys one month and costs a reputation — and in this market reputation travels fast.

07

The statement: cost first, always

Atlas §4.5 wants cost and value in the same message “always in that order”. The order matters and the reason is uncomfortable: leading with the value would be an attempt to soften the number, and she would see it. So 20,000 RWF sits at the top in its own tinted row, and the three value lines follow.

The 89 is the same figure results.html reports, and the footnote says it can be opened. A number on an invoice that cannot be checked is a number that invites suspicion — which is the argument this programme has now made in three separate files.

10

Redesigned for 360, not reflowed

The days headline stacks. A 3.6rem numeral and days left at the rate you have been using it cannot share a baseline at 360, and the sentence is the half that makes the number mean anything.

The amount presets stay two-up rather than stacking. They are the primary action, and a full-width column would push the MoMo number and the PIN warning below the fold — which on this particular screen is a safety regression, not a layout one.

The usage bar goes and the figure stays. 213 / 300 is the information; the bar is a comparison aid.

And the shell's small-screen override is restated, per the trap recorded in conversations.html §07.

11

Open questions, and what the design does about them

Atlas 17.b — how long the grace should be — is unresolved, and the design is built so the answer is a number rather than a rewrite: c7 reads 72 hours from one place and c11 adds to it. 17.d, whether Airtel Money is needed at launch, is why every screen says MoMo and only the drawn MTN prompt names MTN; adding a provider is a second prompt, not a second flow. 17.f, the VAT and Digital Services Tax treatment of prepaid credit, is genuinely open — so no tax line appears anywhere, because inventing one would be worse than omitting it and it will change the displayed amounts when it is confirmed. 17.e — plans versus pure pay-as-you-go — is not settled here; the design shows plans because the atlas does, and the credit card at the top would work identically without them.

And the standing caveat: the Kinyarwanda is carefully written and has not been reviewed by a native speaker. It matters more than usual on c2: the PIN warning is the one piece of copy in this programme whose failure mode is somebody losing money.
