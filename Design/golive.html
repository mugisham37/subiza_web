The decision this flow turns on

You do not believe this because we told you. You believe it because you heard it say your own price down a real phone line.

A browser demo would sound better and prove less. A GSM line is 8 kHz, with real latency and real noise. Testing on the real medium is the honest test precisely because it exposes more.

This test is not allowed to fail

Three routes: we ring you, you talk here, or you dial a number yourself. That is not belt and braces — it is that a person whose test fails does not come back. Nobody adopts a thing they have never heard.

The three questions are hers, not ours

They are drawn from her own price table and her own hours, so the agent is tested on her business rather than on our demo. And they stay on screen during the call, because she will be holding a phone to her ear and will not remember what she meant to ask.

Why the transcript streams

Showing that it understood is the only proof there is. Saying so is not proof.

Research on machine self-assessment reports roughly 40% higher human trust at identical underlying capability. Nothing about the machine improves. What improves is how legible it is.

It introduces the Inbox early

The transcript she reads here is the same transcript she will read every day in Conversations. Meeting it during a test means the real thing teaches her nothing new.

Correction, at the site of the error

A mistake is corrected on the line it happened on, never in a survey afterwards.

A survey asks a person to recall the fault, compress it into options that don't fit, and then asks us to interpret what they picked. Tapping the line itself removes every one of those steps.

Her failure becomes a guard

Any conversation can be kept as a test case. Every time a model, a rule or the knowledge changes, that set runs before the change reaches her. The value is not the mechanism; it is that the same mistake cannot happen to her twice.

What the five are for

Not a score. Five named ways this goes wrong, one card each.

Persona-driven simulation with rubric grading has existed at the enterprise tier for years. Nobody has brought it down to the SME tier. Five named personas on one tap is that idea made usable by somebody who runs a shop.

Two of them run three times

Failing once in three is not failure — it is unreliability, which is harder to live with. The upset customer and the tricky booking are the two that cost a real customer, so they run three times, and “went well” means three out of three. The other three run once.

No score, no percentage

Independent benchmarking puts automated judging near 76% accurate. Publishing “87%” from a component that is wrong a quarter of the time is false precision. When the judge is unsure, this card says so instead.

The only useful way to deliver bad news

What it tried · what happened · why · one button that fixes it.

A card without a button is just bad news. A salon owner has nothing to do with a grade. She has something to do with “add complaining about waiting to the things that fetch you”.

The fix lives in another flow

The change itself is made in the agent's rules. This page names what must change and why, then takes her there. It does not reproduce that surface, because two places to fix one thing is worse than one place.

Only two things block

No escalation target, or you have never heard it. Everything else is advice.

An agent that knows prices and hours is already useful. Blocking on completeness is how products never launch.

A check is not an inventory

“Escalation target set” is an inventory. “We rang +250 788 123 456 and your phone rang” is a check. That distinction is what has cost large firms a great deal of money: they confirmed their controls existed and never modelled them malfunctioning.

No forwarding doesn't block — it empties

We cannot set forwarding on her phone; it is a code she dials herself. So we do not block the decision — we state the truth: you can switch it on now, and not one call will reach it until that is done. The decision stays hers; the honesty is ours.

Why “when you're closed” is the start

Last week 23 calls came in while you were closed. Nineteen rang out.

This is not a safety claim — it is a claim that the decision is smaller. Letting an AI answer calls that currently ring out is a decision with no downside, because the alternative is nobody answering at all.

Rungs are named by consequence

“Calls that come in when you're closed” is a consequence. “Level 2” is our vocabulary. Nobody can consent to something they cannot restate in their own words.

Rung 4 is allowed

A recommendation is not a gate. If she wants it answering everything today, she can — but the card states what that means: her phone stops ringing for customers. That is the one sentence that has to be said plainly, and it is also why we don't recommend it.

Naming the exits

Somebody who can see how to stop is far more willing to start.

Retail research finds leniency raises purchase more than it raises returns, and that the effect comes specifically from money and effort leniency. That is why the exits are listed rather than summarised, and why they are on this screen rather than in a help page.

Pause is one tap, top right

It is not inside the pill's menu. It is its own button beside it, on every screen in this file — including the error screens and the offline one. Pause is safety equipment; putting it one level down is not tidiness, it is a defect.

Silence must never read as broken

Nothing has happened because nobody has called yet — not because it broke.

This screen has to prove it is alive: a heartbeat, a last-checked time, and something to read. None of that is decoration — each one answers the question “is this still working?”

The waiting is where they leave

Someone who switches on at Friday lunchtime may see nothing until Monday. If this screen looks empty they will conclude it is broken and stop it. So it offers her own test call to read — the one piece of real evidence that exists.

The most important moment in the product

The pause completed before the question was asked. The question is not a toll on the way out.

The cancellation canon puts the question before the exit — tell us why, then we'll let you go. This inverts it, which is also how the whole dark-pattern family is defeated by construction rather than by restraint: the decision is already executed before the first word is asked.

One question, not a survey

Across a dataset of more than two million responses, each question beyond the first drops the recovery rate by about 6.7%. And a frustrated owner at nine at night has minimal motivation and maximal difficulty — exactly the conditions that produce meaningless answers. So: few options, well separated, and skippable.

What we did not build

No “are you sure?”, no loss framing, no discount, no “sorry to see you go”. Each of those would also be a lie here: pausing returns calls to her own line.

Evidence, not a timer

“34 calls, no problems” is a reason. “Two weeks” is not.

An upgrade prompt has to be able to cite what it is based on. When it cannot, it should not fire. It fires once, and the permanent refusal is on the same screen rather than buried in settings.

“Don't ask again” means never

Not for six months. Plenty of businesses settle at one rung and that is their decision. Asking again after someone has said no is simply asking again.

Never less reachable than before

When Subiza stops, her phone rings. The line never dies.

This is the hardest rule in the product. Credit gone, our server down, or she stopped it herself — the consequence is identical: calls reach her, exactly as they did before Subiza existed.

Told before, not after

Credit does not run out silently. The message goes at 40 minutes remaining, on WhatsApp and by SMS, and it states the number. A message after the fact is an apology, not a warning.

Topping up is another flow

This screen states what happened and where calls are going right now. It does not draw MoMo and it does not draw prices. It says one sentence and hands over — which is better than reproducing the billing surface here.

Flow 1.10 · atlas F-13 · criticality: existential

Test and go live

The screen where an owner stops evaluating a machine and starts letting it speak to the people who pay her. Twenty screens, and roughly half of them are about the moment she takes it back.

01

Two claims, and only one of them is true

The ladder is routinely sold as a safety mechanism. The evidence does not support that and we should stop saying it. The formal work on progressive autonomy is simulation-only and studies no non-experts; the naturalistic driving studies point the other way, finding that drivers multitask more under partial automation and learn the safeguard sequence well enough to find windows inside it. Clinical alerting shows override rates of 46–96%, and — the finding that matters most — when one site cut alert volume to only the clinically important alerts, acceptance did not significantly improve. Volume reduction alone does not buy attention.

What is supported is the adoption claim. Calibration signals move trust without moving capability: one machine self-assessment study reports ~40% higher human trust at identical underlying performance. The transcript and the persona verdicts are exactly that kind of signal.

So this file never claims the ladder makes the agent safer. It claims only that it makes the decision smaller — which is both true and sufficient. g3's subheading is “This is not one large decision. It is a step,” and no screen anywhere says safer.

02

A check is not an inventory

The readiness list in the atlas reads as field-presence: knows your prices · has a voice · knows who to fetch. Field-presence is exactly the review pattern that has cost large firms very large sums — confirming an inventory of controls exists and never modelling one malfunctioning.

g1 therefore reports observed behaviour wherever it can. “It knows who to fetch” became “We rang you, and your phone rang — +250 788 123 456, today at 11:14”. “Has taken a test call” became “You have heard it speak — a 54-second call”. “Knows your prices” carries “it quoted 3 of them on your test call”. Where we cannot observe, we say so rather than inferring: forwarding is the one row that can only ever be verified by placing a call.

03

Only two things block, and the CTA proves it

No escalation target, and no completed test call. Everything else warns. The test of whether a product means that is whether its primary button is still pressable while warnings are on screen — and here it is, with two warnings visible and the label unchanged.

Forwarding is the interesting case. Atlas §8 says an owner with no forwarding can only run sandbox; atlas §4 says forwarding does not block. Both are right about different things: forwarding does not block the decision, it empties the effect. We cannot set it — it is a GSM code she dials herself — so blocking her would be punishing her for our constraint. The resolution is that rungs stay selectable and the screen states the consequence flatly: you can switch it on now, and not one call will reach it until that is done. The CTA changes its own label to say so.

Recorded deviation. One research stream argued rungs 2–4 should be disabled-with-reason when forwarding is unverified. We didn't take it. Disabling contradicts “warnings do not block”, and a disabled control teaches nothing; a selectable control with an honest consequence line teaches what forwarding is for. If pilot data shows owners going live and then believing the product is broken, this is the first decision to revisit.

04

The recovery moment inverts the cancellation canon

Atlas §8 calls the pause-after-one-bad-call the most important recovery moment in the product, and §11 makes agent.paused within 24 hours of first go-live the highest-priority churn signal we have. The cancellation-UX literature transfers only partially, and the mismatch is the whole design: every documented good practice concerns an exit from commerce. This is an exit from risk. She is not leaving; she is taking back her phone. Under G22 friction here is not merely a dark pattern, it is a defect that leaves a customer talking to something the owner has decided is broken.

So the order is inverted. The pause completes, unconditionally, before a single question is asked, and g9 opens by saying so — destination number included — above the question. There is no gauntlet to resist building because the decision is already executed. One question, five differentiated options, and it is skippable: across a dataset of more than two million responses each additional question drops the recovery rate by ~6.7%, and satisficing theory predicts exactly the failure we would get from a frustrated owner at nine at night.

05

Pause is not in the pill's menu

SC 13.7 requires pause in one tap from any screen. An earlier draft of this file put it inside the pill sheet — tap the pill, tap pause — which is two, and which quietly demotes safety equipment to a menu item. It is now a separate .pausebtn beside the pill in .authhead, present on every screen including the error screens and the offline one. The pill opens a sheet; the pause button does not open anything.

06

Best-of-one flatters, so two personas run three times

Agentic-benchmark work introduced pass^k — the fraction of tasks solved on all k runs — precisely because a single run overstates reliability. The two personas that cost a real customer, the upset customer and the tricky booking, run three times, and “went well” means three out of three. On two out of three the card does not say failed; it says “Once it fetched you; twice it didn't. That isn't failure — it's unreliability, which is harder to live with.” That is pass^k made legible to somebody who runs a shop, and it costs two extra runs.

Three further rulings follow from the same literature. Simulated callers are people-pleasers — an LLM does not stammer, trail off or get confused — so a text-simulated mumbler tests nothing; the mumbler runs pre-recorded 8 kHz narrowband clips through the real recogniser, and its card is tagged Real audio to say that it is a different kind of test. Generative callers make retests incomparable, which would break the whole fix-and-see-it-pass promise, so opening turns are scripted and deterministic. And no score is ever shown: independent benchmarking puts automated judging near 76% accurate, so publishing a percentage from it would be false precision. When the judge is unsure the card says Couldn't judge instead of guessing.

The off-topic caller carries a tag-info reading Policy. W8 bans general-purpose assistants outright, so out-of-scope deflection is a compliance test wearing a quality test's clothes, and the badge says which it is.

What was redesigned for 360, not reflowed

The status pill drops its state dot below 640 and keeps the word: Isubiza iyo ufunze is eighteen characters where After hours is eleven, and a truncated state label is worse than no dot. Colour was never the only signal, so nothing is lost.

Open questions this file does not answer

Atlas 13.a asks whether after-hours-only under-demonstrates value in week one.
