The decision this screen turns on

Somebody part-way through wants one next thing — not a list of everything they haven't done.

A half-finished checklist shows what is done before what is left. That is not flattery — somebody who can see they have started carries on, and somebody who can only see what remains stops.

Zone 1 becomes the checklist

This is not a different page. Home is one screen with four jobs, and in the first of them the job is finishing setup. There is only ever one place to come back to.

The last line is the important one

“No call reaches Subiza until you say so.” The thing that actually stops people finishing setup is not laziness — it is the fear that something will start running before they are ready. Saying plainly that it cannot removes the fear.

Silence must never read as broken

Nothing has happened because nobody has called yet — not because it broke.

Somebody who switches on at Friday lunchtime may see nothing until Monday. If this screen looks empty they will conclude it is broken and stop it — and they will have stopped it for a reason that was never true.

What proves it is alive

A heartbeat, a last-checked time, and something to read. None of it is decoration — each one answers the same single question: is this still working?

Her call, not our sample

What this shows is her own test call, not a sample we wrote. Showing an invented conversation when the only real one is hers would be swapping evidence for decoration.

A feed, not a dashboard

A dashboard shows you everything and makes you find the important part. A feed has already done that work.

Three zones, in an order that never changes. A feed that reorders itself cannot be learned, and the single most valuable thing for somebody returning daily is knowing where to look before they look.

Zone 1 is the only zone with actions

Handled things ask for nothing and are not actionable. The week is a number, not a task. When every part of a feed has a button, nothing means anything needs doing — and at that point the reader stops attending to all of it.

What is allowed into zone 1

Five things only: it fetched you and couldn't reach you · a caller asked for you by name · a booking outside your rules · a caller was upset · a question it couldn't answer. Nothing else. A conversation that went well does not need you, and putting it here would be a lie about what needs you.

The cost of getting it wrong

A false positive costs attention and teaches dismissal. A false negative costs a customer. Those are not equal — but dismissal is the slower poison: once somebody has learned to ignore this, the one real item dies with the rest.

The number is an observation, not a guess

Subiza can prove a call was not answered. It cannot prove a customer was lost. The atlas's “31 you would have missed” claims the second. This screen claims the first — “31 came in while you were closed” — which carries the same meaning, persuades just as well, and cannot be contradicted.

At rung 3 the network itself said so

A call reaches Subiza at rung 3 because a GSM condition fired on her handset — no answer, busy, or unreachable. That is not something we inferred; it is something the network recorded. At rung 4 the handset never rings, no condition fires, and the evidence is not weak — it is absent. Which is why that panel carries no number and says why.

Paused is not an error

She paused it on purpose. This screen does not get to argue.

No red, no “are you sure?”, no counter of days paused, no reminders. The single thing that needs saying is where calls are going right now, and that is the thing it says.

The handled zone stays

What Subiza did before it stopped is not undone by it stopping. Removing it would be punishing her for a decision — and that is precisely what stops people switching it back on.

What every kilobyte costs

For somebody whose data may cost a meaningful share of their income, page weight is a price.

That is why this is a skeleton shaped like the feed rather than a spinner. A spinner answers no question for three seconds; a skeleton says what will be there, so reading starts before the data arrives.

Four states, four different screens

Part of it arrived · you're offline · our side is down · still loading. Each says something different about whether her customers can still reach her, and collapsing them into one “something went wrong” would waste the only distinction that matters.

When our page dies, there is one question

“Can my customers still reach me?” So the error screen answers that before anything else, and does not mention that we are working on it until it has.

A dead channel outranks everything

Every messaging platform's default failure mode is silent. That is why this sits above the feed rather than inside it.

Tokens expire around 60 days and are not auto-renewed. When a channel dies quietly the business stops receiving customers and blames Subiza. Continuous health visibility is the only mitigation there is.

The number is what makes it land

“Instagram isn't working” is information. “4 people have messaged you since then, and were never answered” is a loss. The second one is what makes somebody tap.

She didn't do this

The permission expired because that is what permissions do. Saying so stops the fear that she broke something — and that fear is what stops people acting.

This file draws the product

The eleven files before this are ways in. This is the thing you come back to.

The bottom bar, the rail and the header are settled here once, and every flow after this inherits them as they are. That is why they are designed rather than rushed.

The header is two rows, and that is honest

The atlas asks for six things in the header of every screen: agent status, credit, language, the kill switch, connection health, and the next action. At 360px that is not a layout, it is a wish. So: row one is the greeting and the kill switch; row two is three small chips that scroll. Nothing was dropped and nothing is pretending to fit.

Credit and Connections sit behind More

Not because they matter less — because both are already in the header of every screen. Putting them in the bottom bar would be saying them twice, and the bottom bar has only four slots.

Below 640 the frame stops pretending

On a desktop the frame has a border so the shell can be judged as a shell. On a phone, drawing a phone inside a phone is a lie about what you are holding — so the border goes and the frame becomes the page.

Home

The daily return, and the first file in this programme to draw the actual product rather than a way into it. Four lifecycle states, three zones, one shell that eight later flows inherit.

01

The shell has never been drawn before

Eleven files precede this one and every one of them is a wizard inside .auth — a single centred column with a heading and a next button. .applayout, .appnav, .appbody and .tabbar exist in the shared foundation, but only as gallery specimens at min-height:340px with a 224px rail. Nothing has ever used them.

So this file settles the shell, and Conversations, Escalation, Results, Credit, Team, Compliance and Notifications all inherit what it settles. Three decisions were made deliberately rather than by default:

The frameA bordered app surface with its own header, scroll and navigation, with a Phone / Desktop toggle in the studio toolbarA responsive law that is asserted in prose is not a law. Both forms are on screen and switchable, so the claim is checkable
The headerTwo rows. Greeting and kill switch, then a scrolling strip of three state chipsAtlas §8 asks for six things in one header. At 360px that is a wish, not a layout. Two rows drops nothing and hides nothing
The bottom barHome · Chats · Agent · More — credit and connections behind MoreAtlas open question 2.b asks whether Credit should be permanent. It already is — it is a chip in the header of every screen. A bottom-bar slot would be saying it twice out of four
02

A feed, not a dashboard

A dashboard shows everything and leaves the reader to find the important part. A feed has already done that work. Three zones in an order that never changes, because a feed that reorders itself cannot be learned — and the most valuable property for somebody returning every day is knowing where to look before they look.

Zone 1 is the only zone with actions. Handled things ask for nothing. The week is a number, not a task. When every region of a screen carries a button, nothing on it signals that anything needs doing, and the reader stops attending to all of it.

Not admitted: conversations that went well, anything already dealt with, anything informational, and anything whose only claim is that it happened recently. 03

The number, and why the atlas's version is not shippable

Atlas §3.4 marks one line ← THE NUMBER and §3.2 says Results is where the subscription renews. The line is: “31 you would have missed.”

That is a counterfactual. It asserts what would have happened in a world that did not occur, and it is load-bearing for revenue — exactly the kind of claim that should not be a guess. One sentence governs the whole zone:

Subiza can prove a call was not answered. Subiza cannot prove a customer was lost.

The atlas's headline claims the second. The fix is to claim the first — the observation that entails it:

RungAtlas phrasingWhat this file saysStatus
2 — after hours31 you would have missed31 of them came in while you were closedObservation. Unfalsifiable, same meaning
3 — calls you miss—19 of them you didn't pick upObservation. We watched the phone not be answered
4 — everything—We can't tell you what you'd have missedUnknowable, and said so
Week one—Too early to tell you anythingSix conversations is not a trend

“31 came in while you were closed” carries precisely the same implication as “31 you would have missed”, persuades exactly as well, and cannot be contradicted by a customer who says actually I check my phone in the evenings. The counterfactual can.

And at rung 3 the claim is stronger than an observation — it is a routing fact. Conditional forwarding (Flow 07 §4) sets **61* no-answer, **67* busy, **62* unreachable. A call only reaches Subiza because one of those conditions fired on her handset. Nothing was inferred; the carrier switch made a decision and recorded it. That puts this number in a different class from every we saved you X counter in the category, which multiply an event count by an undisclosed constant or run a correlational attribution model — and the published work on real holdouts shows attribution without a control is inflated in a known direction.

Rung 4 is the honest casualty. With unconditional forwarding (**21*) the handset never rings, no condition fires, and the evidence is not weak — it is absent. So that panel carries no number and explains why. That costs a line of marketing and buys something that cannot be bought back.

The number's only defence is per-item auditability. A count that grows with traffic whether or not the product is any good is structurally the weak kind of metric, and the one thing that redeems it is that every unit behind it can be opened. So the claim line is a control, not a caption — tapping it goes to the items. It also means no RWF figure appears anywhere on this screen: converting conversations into money requires a constant only Claudine can supply, and inventing one would be the exact failure this whole section exists to avoid.

Recorded correction to the atlas. This is the third in the programme, after the verification-vs-checkbox claim in Flow 11 and the single-quality-badge model in Flow 12. The atlas's intent — put the value on the home screen, above the fold, every visit — is kept in full. Only the grammar of the claim changes.

04

Two rules this file broke in draft

G1 — one next action. The first build gave every zone 1 card a lime primary button, straight from the atlas mock at §3.4. With five cards in the queue that is five recommendations, which is none. Only the top card in the queue now carries a primary; the rest step down to secondary. The queue is ordered, so the one recommended next step is the one at the top — which is also how somebody actually works through a list.

G7 — skip and resume, always. Tapping Done removed the card and said nothing, which reads as deleting something. It now says Moved to Conversations. The item leaves this screen; it does not leave the product, and the difference between clearing a queue and losing something is one line of copy.

05

Zone 1 collapsing is the whole argument

Atlas §3.4: “If nothing needs the owner, zone 1 collapses to a single line and does not occupy space. This is the difference between a product that respects attention and one that manufactures anxiety.”

The claim holds, and the mechanism is habituation. A count shown every day stops being read as a count and starts being read as furniture — the same path by which clinical alerting reaches override rates in the high tens of percent. The defence is not a gentler badge; it is that the badge is absent when there is nothing, so its presence stays information rather than decoration.

The collapsed line is deliberately unglamorous: one row, a tick, six words. No illustration, no “great work!”, no streak, no confetti. It takes a line because it earned a line.

06

The four lifecycle states against the eight

Home has four lifecycle states of its own (atlas §3.4) and must also carry the eight states from Flow 24 §6. These are orthogonal axes, which would be 32 screens if taken literally. They are not taken literally: the lifecycle states are what Home is for today, and the eight are what went wrong fetching it. Only the combinations that differ meaningfully are drawn.

StateWhere it lives
Loadingx1 — a skeleton shaped like the feed. At the 3G budget a spinner answers nothing for three seconds
Emptyh2 ready and listening, and zone 1's collapsed line in h3. Two different emptinesses, two different answers
Offlinex3 — leads with the fact that the agent is unaffected, then stamps the data with when it was true
Partialx2 — zone 1 arrived and is correct; the week's numbers didn't, and we will not show a number we are unsure of
Permission deniedNot drawn. Home requires no device permission; the roles that cannot see Home cannot sign in at all (Flow 18)

Rate limitedNot drawn. A feed read is not an action a user can exhaust

Three of the eight are deliberately not drawn, with the reason given rather than a screen invented to satisfy a checklist. Inventing a rate-limit state for a home feed would be designing for the audit, not the owner.

08

Redesigned for 360, not reflowed

The app frame loses its border and its fixed height below 640 and becomes the page itself. On a desktop the border lets the shell be judged as a shell; on a phone, drawing a phone inside a phone is a lie about what the reader is holding.

The state strip scrolls horizontally rather than truncating. A truncated state label — Answering after ho… — is worse than one the reader has to nudge, and at this width Kinyarwanda's Yakira iyo mufunze and English's Answering after hours differ by enough to break any fixed allocation.

The action row on a zone 1 card wraps to two rows rather than shrinking. Three buttons at 360 would each fall below the tap-target floor, and a button too small to hit reliably is not a smaller button, it is a broken one.

The rail is not a narrow rail at mid widths. It is present with labels, or it is the bottom bar — icon-only navigation with seven destinations is a memory test.

Open questions this file takes a position on

Atlas 2.b — bottom bar of four, or is Credit permanent? Answered: Credit is permanent, as a header chip, and the bar stays at four. Atlas 2.a (Results vs Analytics) is untested and this file uses Results without claiming it is validated. Atlas 2.d — whether owners want the customer timeline — is Flow 14's to settle, not Home's.

A standing gap, not specific to this file. G15 requires four languages switchable anywhere. Every prototype in this programme carries two, RW and EN, on a studio-toolbar control rather than an in-product one. That is a prototype convention and it has now been inherited twelve times; when the shell becomes real the switcher belongs in the header with all four.

And the caveat every file in this programme carries: the Kinyarwanda is carefully written and has not been reviewed by a native speaker. Atlas SC 6.12 requires that walkthrough.
