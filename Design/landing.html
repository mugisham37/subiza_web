The questions people actually ask

Fair questions.

Will my customers know it's a machine?

Yes — because we tell them. Subiza opens every call by saying it's an assistant, in the caller's own language. That's partly the law arriving across several markets, and partly the point: what frustrates a customer is not knowing they're talking to software, it's ringing out to nobody. You can hear the exact wording on the demo number above.

What if it says something wrong?

Subiza only answers from what you gave it. When the question falls outside that, it doesn't improvise — it says it can't confirm and fetches you. You also get a list of every question it couldn't answer, so the gaps become things you fix rather than things you find out about from an angry customer. And you set hard limits: "never quote a price for colour treatments" is a rule it will not break.

Do I lose my number?

No. Nothing about your number changes — no porting, no new SIM, no new handset. You're switching on call forwarding, which is a setting on your line, and you switch it off again by dialling ##61#. We show you that code at the same moment we show you the one that turns it on.

What if my credit runs out?

Your phone rings, exactly as it did before Subiza existed. That's the foundational promise of the whole product, and it holds for every failure — out of credit, our servers down, a bad night on the network. Subiza must never make a business less reachable than it was before. We warn you at 20% and again at 5%, and we extend your grace period automatically if the fault was ours.

Where does my customers' data go?

Personal data is stored in Rwanda, as Article 50 of Law 058/2021 requires. Recordings are off by default and are your choice, not ours. Your customers can ask us what we hold about them and we answer within 30 days, and you can delete a conversation, or your entire account, whenever you want. The full privacy notice spells out every one of those routes.

Can I turn it off?

One code, instantly, without asking us. There's no contract to exit, no card to cancel, no monthly subscription that quietly renews — credit is prepaid, like airtime, and unused credit doesn't disappear. If Subiza isn't earning its keep you should be able to stop in ten seconds, and you can.

Will this work on my phone and my network?

Call forwarding is a standard GSM feature, so it works on MTN and Airtel and on any handset that can dial — including a feature phone. It needs no internet on your side at all: the call is answered on our servers, not on your device. That also means Subiza keeps answering when your own data has run out.

How it works

No magic. A phone code that has existed since 1993.

This page explains the whole mechanism, including the parts that are inconvenient for us. You should know exactly what you're switching on before you switch it on.

You choose when Subiza picks up

Two options, and we recommend the first. Only when you don't answer — your phone rings for 20 seconds first, and Subiza picks up only if you don't, or if you're already on another call. Or every call, if you'd rather never be interrupted and be fetched only when a customer needs a decision.

You dial a code on the phone holding the SIM

We show it, we let you copy it, and we open your dialler for you. What we cannot do is dial it for you. A forwarding code is a command from your SIM to your carrier — it cannot be sent from a website, and it cannot be sent by us. That's a limitation, and it's also the reason you stay in control of your own line.

Dial this
**61*250788456123*11*20#

Forward after 20 seconds of ringing. Your phone still rings first, every time.

Copy the codeOpen dialler

Android only, and it never dials by itself. On iPhone we give you copy-and-paste steps instead, because tap-to-dial links quietly fail there.

We call your number to check it actually worked

We don't trust that it worked — we test it. And we re-test it every week, because forwarding can be silently cleared by a carrier reset, a SIM swap or a handset change, and the first way most people find out is a customer complaining. If it stops, you get a message with the code to re-dial.

You tell it about your business

Hours, location, prices, languages, the voice it uses, and — the part that matters most — the limits. "Never quote a price for colour treatments. Take a message instead." Those rules are absolute; the agent does not weigh them against being helpful.

It answers, and hands you the ones that need you

When a customer needs a person, Subiza calls you — a ringing phone, not a notification competing with everything else in your tray. No answer in 20 seconds, it messages you on WhatsApp with the caller's number in the text. Still nothing, it tries the next person on your rota. If nobody is reachable, it takes a message and gives the customer a specific time, which then appears at the top of your day.

About

Subiza means “answer”.

It's the Kinyarwanda imperative of gusubiza — to answer, to reply. The related noun, igisubizo, means both "an answer" and "a solution", which is a coincidence we didn't deserve but will happily take.

The thing we kept noticing

Small businesses here don't lose customers because their work is bad. They lose them because nobody picked up. A call rings out at seven in the evening. A WhatsApp message sits unread until Monday. A comment asking "how much?" is never answered. The owner is doing sales, delivery, accounting and support at the same time, and the standard fix — a person whose job is to answer — costs more per month than many of these businesses clear.

Rwanda has 278,060 enterprises. Four in five of them have between one and three people. When that person steps out to the market, the entire business goes quiet — and every missed call is a customer who has already dialled somebody else by the time they get back.

What we believe

Kinyarwanda first, not eventually. Most voice AI is built in English and localised afterwards, which is why it mishandles the language people actually use. We started at the other end, on 8 kHz narrowband audio, in the noise of a real shop, because that is the only place the product has to work.

Never guess. A confident wrong answer damages your business; an admitted gap only frustrates a caller for a moment. When Subiza doesn't know, it says so and fetches a person. We would rather be occasionally unhelpful than routinely wrong.

Never make you less reachable. Whatever fails — our servers, your credit, the network — calls fall through to your own phone exactly as they did before. Every other decision in the product gives way to that one, and we test it as an acceptance criterion rather than assuming it.

Say the inconvenient thing. Meta's verification takes 5–15 working days. We cannot dial your forwarding code for you. Our prices aren't final. All three of those are on this website in plain type, because a product that starts by managing your expectations is easier to trust than one that starts by exceeding them on paper.

Where we are

We're in pilot in Kigali. We don't have thousands of customers or a wall of enterprise logos, and we're not going to borrow either. The only thing we can genuinely show you is the agent itself, which is why the demo number is at the top of every page rather than behind a form.
