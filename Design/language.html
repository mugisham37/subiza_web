The decision this flow turns on

The language is decided by the caller, in the first three seconds. Detection assists that decision — it never replaces it.

Detection is unreliable technology. When it fails, the person who finds out is the customer — which is the worst possible place for a failure to appear.

One badge per language would be a lie

A phone call carries about half the sound a voice note does. The same language can be understood well in messages and be hard work on the phone. So every language here carries two marks — the phone and the message — each telling its own truth.

"Not yet measured" is a label, not a gap

The atlas gives three labels: good, improving, not yet available for voice. A fourth is needed, because before we measure, none of those three is true. Showing "good" for Kinyarwanda before anyone has measured it is making a promise to a customer who isn't ours.

The two that are never a setting

If the caller asks for a language, it is obeyed. If they press a key, it is obeyed. There is no control to switch that off, because a caller who asks twice and is answered otherwise is a caller who has learned they're not talking to a person. So the first two rows are locked — the same grammar as the locked withdrawal control on the voice owner's record.

There is no detection dial

We could have shipped a detection-sensitivity slider. We didn't, because nobody knows how often Rwandan business callers mix languages — so there is no threshold we could honestly recommend. The only control is the three modes, and each one states what it costs you in plain words rather than as a number that means nothing.

One agent, many tongues

Most products let you create an agent per language. Three months later they have drifted: one knows the new price, the other doesn't. This page is one reading of one configuration — the wording changes, the rules never do. There is no "create a French agent" button here, and there never will be.

A machine translation is a draft until a person says otherwise

A machine doesn't know your business says "murakaza neza" rather than "mwaramutse". So every line carries an amber dot until you confirm it — and the length is shown in spoken seconds, not characters. A long greeting is the single most common reason callers start talking over the agent.

The one number worth watching

Switches the caller didn't ask for. Everything else on this page is context; this is the number that tells you whether your setting is right. An agent that changes language for no reason is a mistake the customer hears immediately — and across the industry it happens on roughly one call in twenty. The target is printed on screen, because a target you can't see isn't one.

Under thirty calls, we show you a sentence instead of a number

A percentage from six calls is noise wearing the costume of a fact. A shop owner who sees 83% on a screen believes it, and we'd have lied to her. So under thirty we remove the number and put a sentence in its place saying we don't know. That's also why this page states when measuring started and on how many calls.

Mixing languages is not an event

"Ndashaka kubona appointment kuwa gatandatu" is one sentence, not two. It renders as one turn, with two language chips and a dotted underline under the English word — and no switch marker above it. Drawing normal speech as an event tells somebody the way they talk is a mistake.

A switch marker carries what caused it

Asked, pressed a key, or detected on its own. Only the last can be a mistake, and only the last is counted as one. And when a switch arrives right after a failure to understand, we don't call it a preference — we count it separately and say we don't yet know what it is. Atlas question 12.e is open, so the screen doesn't answer it.

A suggestion made of evidence, not of selling

We don't say "turn on Swahili". We say five people called and spoke Swahili, and we show what they said and what happened. The decision is yours, and if you don't take it we won't ask again this month. A suggestion built from your own traffic is advice; the same suggestion repeated is a sales pitch.

We never quietly substitute

If a caller speaks a language you haven't enabled, the agent apologises in your primary language and fetches you. It doesn't attempt it. It doesn't approximate it. And where a language works in messages but has no voice, that is said on this page rather than discovered by a customer.

The voice note beats the phone call

This surprises people. A phone line carries roughly 300 to 3,400 Hz; a WhatsApp voice note carries considerably more. So the same customer, saying the same thing, is understood better in a message than on the phone. That's why the quality labels differ by channel — and why we never merge them into one.

Answering in the form they chose

Someone who sent a voice note did it for a reason — typing Kinyarwanda is work, or they were on a moto. Answering only in text answers them in a form they didn't choose. So they get both, in the same language, and that isn't configurable.

Flow 1.9 · atlas F-12

Language and switching

The flow where the product's whole differentiation lives, and the one where the owner configures something she will never hear. Nine screens, and an argument about what we are allowed to claim.

01

Choice first, detection second

Automatic language detection is unreliable for closely related and heavily mixed languages, and the failure is customer-visible. One vendor documents roughly 4% of calls unexpectedly switching language around the eighth turn; another recommends avoiding auto-detection altogether in favour of routing to language-specific agents — which is the cure that causes the disease the atlas forbids at L3.

The position, and every screen follows from it: the caller decides, in the first three seconds, by one of three means, and detection assists that decision rather than replacing it. An explicit request is absolute. A keypad press is absolute. Detection is advisory and only above threshold. Those first two render as locked rows on l3, in the same visual grammar as the locked withdrawal control in voice.html — a control that is present and does not press teaches whose decision it is better than a sentence does.

There is no detection dial, deliberately. Atlas question 12.b — how heavily Rwandan business calls actually code-switch — is unanswered, so there is no threshold we could honestly recommend. Shipping a slider would be asking the owner to tune a number neither of us understands. The only control is the three modes, and each states its cost in plain words.

02

What we are allowed to say about a language

The atlas asks for honest per-language quality labels and gives three: Good · Improving · Not yet available for voice. Two changes were necessary.

First, every language needs two labels, not one. A phone line carries roughly 300–3,400 Hz; a voice note carries considerably more. The same language can be good in messages and hard work on a call — and atlas §8 says thresholds are tuned per channel, not globally. A single badge per language would be a lie, so the board shows a phone mark and a message mark, each lit or struck through.

Second, a fourth rung is required: Not yet measured. Before Phase 0, none of the three atlas labels is true for Kinyarwanda on a telephone line. This is not an edge case — it is the shipping state.

03

One agent, translated views

Atlas constraint L3: separate prompts per language drift apart — one configuration, translated views, never separate agents per language. Three months after you create a French agent, one of them knows the new price and the other doesn't.

So l4 is a view, not a second configuration. Each line shows the target language first — because the target is the thing being judged — with the Kinyarwanda source quiet beneath it. This deliberately inverts the transcript component, where the original always leads and the translation sits under it; there the record is what was actually said, here the draft is what needs approving. Every string carries an amber dot until a person confirms it, and the length is stamped in spoken seconds, not characters, because a long greeting is the most common cause of callers talking over the agent. There is no affordance anywhere to create a language-specific agent.

04

Code-switching is not an event

"Ndashaka kubona appointment kuwa gatandatu" is one sentence in two languages, and in Kigali it is the normal case rather than the exception. The atlas is explicit: this is not a language switch and must not be treated as one.

The rendering carries the whole argument. A mixed utterance is one turn, with two language chips and a dotted underline beneath the foreign span — and no switch marker above it. A switch marker is reserved for a sustained change, and it carries the trigger that caused it: asked keypad detected. Only the third can be a mistake, and only the third is counted as one.

What this design cannot promise. SC 12.2 asks for >95% of code-switched utterances not triggering a full switch. Handling code-switching is stage K5 of the Kinyarwanda programme, scheduled for Phase 3 — it is roadmap, not shipped. The screens render the correct behaviour and the evidence row for it ships in an unmeasured state. The design can show what right looks like; it cannot make the model do it.

05

The number that matters, and the number we refuse to show

Atlas §12 names it: language.switch with trigger = detection and no preceding caller switch. That is the spurious-switch rate, and it is the single most important quality figure in this flow. It gets the headline on l5, in words rather than jargon — "switches the caller didn't ask for" — with its target, under 1% of calls, printed beside it.

The refusal is the other half. Below thirty calls the meter is removed and replaced with a sentence, not greyed out and not shown at low confidence. A percentage computed from six calls is noise wearing the costume of a fact, and a shop owner who reads 83% on a screen will believe it. The page also states when measuring began and on how many calls — because a number with no denominator is not evidence.

Two more are counted separately rather than folded into an average. Times and numbers: "Saturday saa tatu" — English weekday, Kinyarwanda clock — is how people book here, and when it resolves wrongly a booking goes in wrong; an aggregate understanding rate hides exactly that. And switches that arrive right after a failure, which atlas 12.e leaves open: we do not classify them as preference or repair, we count them in their own row and say we don't know.

06

The gate we have not passed

Atlas 12.a blocks everything in this flow, and it is unanswered. The real Kinyarwanda understanding rate on 8 kHz telephone audio is unknown. The project documentation is blunt about it: no reliable published WER figure exists for any candidate model on telephone audio, and "that absence is itself the finding." The only quantified expectation is a hedged range — narrowband degradation informally cited at 1.5–3× relative — and the only hard Kinyarwanda number in the corpus is a cautionary one from a deliberately undersized fine-tune. Phase 0 exists to produce one honest number.

The design carries that three ways rather than working around it. Kinyarwanda's call state defaults to "Not yet measured", and that is the screen's shipping state. The evidence page ships with its empty state designed first, worded as ignorance rather than as zero. And l2 says it in one sentence a shop owner can read: nobody has published this number for any speech system, including ours. That is G13 — say "I don't know" rather than guess — applied to the product's own capability rather than to a caller's question.

10

Where this flow stops

The agent's rules, tone and greeting text belong to Flow 09 and are shipped in agent.html — this surface shows a translated view and deep-links to the source. The pronunciation dictionary is Flow 10, shipped in knowledge.html; language links to it and does not reimplement it. Voice selection, availability and cloning are Flow 11, shipped in voice.html; this flow consumes voice availability to draw the chat-only state and nothing more. The full conversation inbox is Flow 14 and is not yet designed — so l6 and l7 show three-to-five turn excerpts, reached from evidence rows, and carry no filters, no search and no reply affordance. Escalation is Flow 15: the ladder on l3 ends by handing over and stops. Analytics is Flow 16, and the fleet-wide view of flap-locking is Flow 22, in the admin console. None of those are drawn here.
