Flow 1.14 · atlas F-16 · blocks renewal

Results

Where the subscription renews, and where it is lost silently. Fifteen screens, one number, and a great deal of care about what that number is allowed to claim.

01

The failure mode is silence, not dissatisfaction

Atlas §1: “The product works, the owner never notices, and they cancel while describing it as ‘not really doing much’.” That is a different problem from a product people dislike, and it has a different fix — not persuasion, but visibility of something specific enough to remember.

02

The exclusions are the design

Atlas §2 carries the sentence this whole file is built around:

A number the owner can poke a hole in is worse than a smaller number they trust. If they ever catch us inflating it, every other number in the product becomes suspect.

Two consequences, both structural rather than editorial. First, the headline is a control. SC 16.5 requires every counted conversation to be listed and openable, so the number is a button and v2 is the list. A count that grows with traffic whether the product is good or not has exactly one defence, and this is it.

Second, the exclusions get their own screen — v3, reachable from the headline — and they are itemised with counts, including the awkward one: 3 of your own test calls, not counted. Showing the subtractions is the only way a total means anything.

03

Carrying Home's correction forward

Atlas §2 words the headline as “31 calls you would have missed”. home.html corrected that to the observation that entails it — “31 came in while you were closed” — and this file keeps the correction, for the same reason plus a new one: the atlas's own arithmetic already agrees. Its inclusion table is entirely observational (arrived outside hours · line was busy · rang unanswered), and its exclusion table rules out rung-4 calls explicitly because they are “unprovable”. The counterfactual survives only in the headline's grammar, not in the sum.

RungHeadlineBasis

Rung 4 is where an honest product loses a marketing line. v10 says it out loud: we could write 63 as calls you'd have missed; it would not be true, and the day you worked that out every other number would become suspect. Merging the two figures would be inflation you could not see — which is how trust actually dies, not by being caught lying but by realising you could have been.

04

Atlas 16.b, answered: no money unless she supplies it

The atlas asks whether the headline should be revenue — more persuasive, but it needs an average transaction value we cannot verify. Both horns are bad: a made-up multiplier is the single most destructive number we could print, and refusing to talk about money at all leaves the most motivating framing on the table.

So we ask her. v12 offers one optional field — what does one job bring in, roughly? — and if she fills it, the arithmetic appears with its source on its face: 9 × 15,000 Frw, the figure you gave on 12 September. Not ours, and not estimated. If she doesn't, no money figure exists anywhere in the product. The persuasive version becomes available without us inventing anything.

05

The intent ranking, and why it is the best screen here

Atlas §4.2 calls it “the most valuable screen in the product for the business itself”, and the reason is that it is not a product metric at all — it is new information about her own business that no Rwandan SME has previously had.

The rows that matter most are the ones Subiza could not answer. They are demand, already quantified: three people asked about parking is a two-minute task, where add more answers is an unanswerable request. And the screen states that when the same question defeats many salons it is our template's fault rather than hers — because hiding that implies the fault was hers.

06

Plain statements, and bad news with a fix

Atlas §4.3 asks for statements rather than metrics, and the reason is that an owner has nothing to do with a rate and something to do with a sentence. “Subiza handled 8 out of 10 on its own”, never containment rate: 80%. Latency appears only when it degrades — a measure that is always present is furniture; one that appears when it breaks is a signal.

And weakness is shown with the remedy already attached: “3 people spoke Swahili and Subiza couldn't help them” plus a button that adds Swahili. The alternative is an owner who discovers it from an angry customer, at which point it is not information, it is humiliation.

Containment is the band from escalation.html, unchanged, with both edges live. 95% would not be a triumph.

07

Zero traffic is not a quiet week

The single highest-value edge case in the atlas, §9: zero traffic often means broken forwarding, not a quiet week. So the weekly message has three shapes, and the third is not a report at all:

WeekWhat goes out
NormalThe number, one supporting fact, one thing customers asked. 38 words
Quiet“Quiet week — 4 conversations… your busiest day is usually Saturday.” Honest, plus something useful
ZeroNo number and no encouragement. “No calls came through this week. Let's check your forwarding is still on.”

Sending “quiet week!” over a dead line is the worst message this product could produce, and it is also the easiest one to produce by accident — a template that always leads with a figure will happily print a zero.

08

The message, and the platform rules it lives inside

Delivered on WhatsApp because it reaches her where she already is, costs her no data, and is itself an instance of the thing she pays for — an AI writing a clear WhatsApp message. Under 60 words, with the count rendered on the mock so the constraint is visible rather than aspirational.

W6 makes this business-initiated, so it needs an approved template; W18 means the categorisation has to be right or it is rejected. The screen states plainly that this is a utility message — an account update about her own service — because that is both true and the condition of it being delivered at all. W19's per-message billing from 1 October 2026 leaves one message per tenant per week comfortably inside the free allowance, but it belongs in the cost model rather than on this screen.

The off switch is one tap, and v13 carries the two sentences that make it honest: switching the message off does not switch off the numbers, and switching it off is information we want rather than a loss to be argued with.

11

Redesigned for 360, not reflowed

The headline stacks. A 4rem numeral and a two-line label cannot share a baseline at 360, and the label is the half carrying the meaning — came in while you were closed is the entire claim.

The intent bar disappears below 640 and the count stays. The bar is a comparison aid; the number is the information, and dropping the right one is the whole skill.

The gap label wraps to its own line rather than squeezing between a name and a count, because it didn't know is the reason the row is interesting.

12

Open questions this file does not settle

Atlas 16.a — whether the counterfactual survives translation into Kinyarwanda — is partly dissolved by the change above: an observation translates cleanly where a counterfactual does not, which is a second argument for it that we did not go looking for. 16.c, whether Monday morning competes with the start-of-week rush, is untested; the time is configurable and the screen says so. 16.d — whether owners want intent rankings or whether it is an analyst's idea of value — is the biggest open question in this file, and the honest answer is that we find it compelling and have not proven she will. 16.f, whether Meta approves the template as a utility message in Rwanda, is a delivery risk this design cannot mitigate; if it is refused, the SMS fallback becomes the primary channel and the 60-word limit becomes a 160-character one.

And the standing caveat: the Kinyarwanda is carefully written and has not been reviewed by a native speaker. Atlas SC 16.12 requires the report to render correctly in all four languages; two are wired here, and G15 asks for four.
